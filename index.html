02 / Research question

What does access to electricity reveal about development?

The central analysis examines the association between GDP per capita and electricity access across countries and years. A broader set of indicators adds context on health, education, infrastructure and the energy transition.

Central question

How is electricity access associated with economic development across countries, and how does this relationship vary over time?

03 / Research journey

From raw indicators to interpretable evidence

A reproducible pipeline was built in Python. Each stage preserves the source data while creating auditable intermediate datasets and analytical outputs.

STAGE 01

Data collection

Retrieved 15 World Bank World Development Indicators through the API for 2000–2023. Each series was saved separately in its raw CSV file.

Output · data/raw/
STAGE 02

Data cleaning

Standardized column names, sorted country-year records and created clean copies. Raw files were preserved; missing values were retained as NaN.

Output · data/clean/
STAGE 03

Panel construction

Combined the 15 indicator files using outer joins on country name, country code and year, retaining records when some indicators were unavailable.

Output · world_bank_panel.csv
STAGE 04

Quality assessment

Reviewed dimensions, data types, missingness, entity coverage and key uniqueness. Built a 10-indicator analytical subset for focused analyses.

Output · quality reports + analysis panel
STAGE 05

Statistical analysis

Conducted univariable summaries, Pearson and Spearman correlations, selected scatterplots, yearly comparisons and country-clustered panel regressions.

Output · tables + model estimates
STAGE 06

Visualization & interpretation

Created correlation heatmaps, scatterplot grids, coefficient plots and regional comparisons to communicate patterns and analytical limitations.

Output · figures + dashboard
04 / Data & methods

One integrated panel, multiple analytical lenses

The full panel retains all 15 indicators. Country-level analyses exclude aggregate entities with missing country codes; missing indicator values are handled within each analysis rather than deleting entire rows globally.

Data architecture

  • Full panel: 6,916 rows × 18 columns.
  • Analytical subset: 6,916 rows × 13 columns.
  • 261 unique non-null country codes and 130 aggregate rows.
  • Country-level and aggregate country-year keys were separately validated: zero duplicates.

Descriptive & bivariate analysis

  • Univariable statistics and indicator-level missingness.
  • Pearson correlation for linear association.
  • Spearman rank correlation for monotonic association.
  • Pairwise sample sizes reported because coverage differs by indicator.

Panel inference

  • Pooled OLS with country-clustered standard errors.
  • Country and year fixed effects with country-clustered standard errors.
  • Seven selected relationships estimated under both specifications.
  • Fixed effects account for time-invariant country differences and common year effects.

Regional analysis

  • World Bank region and income metadata joined to the analytical panel.
  • Yearly and overall electricity–life expectancy summaries.
  • Aggregate entities and records without region excluded from regional comparisons.
  • Small regional country counts are explicitly treated with caution.
Interpretation boundary. These are observational associations, not causal effects. Repeated country-year observations require care with dependence; country-clustered standard errors address within-country dependence, but do not eliminate time-varying confounding or cross-country dependence.

06 / Selected findings

Patterns are strong—but not uniform

The analysis shows consistent positive associations between electricity access and several development outcomes. The strength and interpretation of those relationships depend on the measure and model.

+0.384 Pooled Pearson · GDP & electricity
+0.847 Pooled Spearman · GDP & electricity
+0.330 Yearly Pearson · 2023
6,051 Paired country-year observations

GDP and electricity access

The pooled Pearson correlation is +0.3841, while Spearman is +0.8473. The difference suggests a stronger monotonic than linear relationship in the pooled observations. Yearly Pearson correlations declined from +0.4412 in 2000 to +0.3301 in 2023.

Within-country estimates

For electricity access and log GDP per capita, the country-and-year fixed-effects estimate is 0.00648. This is a within-country association after accounting for common year effects—not a causal effect.

How to interpret the coefficients. Pearson's r measures the strength and direction of a linear association. Spearman's ρ measures the strength and direction of a rank-based monotonic association. A difference between the two may indicate nonlinearity, skewed distributions or sensitivity to extreme values. Neither establishes causality.

Pairwise sample sizes vary because indicator availability differs across countries and years. The pooled country-year observations are repeated observations, not independent country-level samples.

Access is associated with development. Context matters.

The strongest descriptive patterns connect electricity access with health, basic services and digital connectivity. Yet pooled comparisons mix differences between countries with changes within countries. The panel specifications help separate these perspectives, while still stopping short of causal claims.
